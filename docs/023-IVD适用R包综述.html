23  R Packages for IVD Data Analysis: A Review

IVD data analysis is broader than analytical-performance evaluation. A complete study may involve reagent and instrument performance, diagnostic accuracy, observer agreement, reference intervals, clinical prediction, follow-up outcomes, evidence synthesis, and high-dimensional biomarker data. This chapter provides a review of CRAN packages that support these tasks.

23.1 Scope and Inclusion Rules

This review includes packages available from CRAN by 2026-09-01 that directly target IVD/laboratory medicine or provide core methods used in IVD research. It also includes foundational or recommended packages such as stats, survival, MASS, and nlme. It does not attempt to list every general data-wrangling, plotting, database, or arbitrary statistics package, and it excludes packages available only from Bioconductor, GitHub, or other repositories. Package availability, versions, defaults, and maintenance status can change; inclusion is not a quality or regulatory endorsement.

23.2 Classification Framework

Analysis level Typical question Sections
Analytical performance Is the method precise, linear, stable, unbiased, and sensitive? Method comparison, precision, calibration/sensitivity, stability/QC
Clinical performance Can the test distinguish states, and how should a cutoff or equivalence be evaluated? Diagnostic accuracy, ROC, paired comparison, agreement
Clinical utility Does the result improve risk stratification, decisions, or long-term prediction? Regression, prediction, survival, decision curves
Evidence synthesis How should sensitivity and specificity from several studies be combined? Diagnostic-accuracy and general meta-analysis
Biomarker discovery What structure and discrimination signals exist in multi-marker or high-dimensional data? Multivariate analysis, chemometrics, machine learning
Study support How should missing data, outliers, repeats, sample size, and reproducibility be handled? Design, data validation, reporting, audit

23.3.6 Stability, Interference, Recovery, Lot Differences, and QC

ivdtools provides stability regression, interference/recovery, dilution, lot bias, uncertainty, and Westgard QC components. General-purpose alternatives include stats, nlme, lme4, emmeans, multcomp, segmented, strucchange, changepoint, qcc, IQCC, qicharts2, SixSigma, spc, metRology, propagate, errors, and units. These tools can support time trends, mixed models, marginal comparisons, control charts, and uncertainty propagation, but study-specific acceptance limits and design remain external.

23.4.1 Binary Metrics, ROC, and Cutoffs

Cutoff selection must be prespecified and externally or internally validated. A cutoff optimized and evaluated on the same data has optimism.

23.4.2 Paired Tests, Multiclass, No Gold Standard, and Agreement

DTComPair, testCompareR, and CompareTests compare paired binary tests. bcROCsurface and ROCpsych support multiclass ROC. poLCA, randomLCA, and BayesLCA support latent-class models when no perfect reference exists. vcd, DescTools, irr, and irrCAC support Kappa and categorical agreement; psych, irr, and SimplyAgree support ICC and continuous agreement. RJafroc and MRMCaov support multi-reader multi-case designs.

23.5 Diagnostic Accuracy and General Meta-Analysis

23.5.1 Diagnostic-Accuracy Meta-Analysis

mada provides diagnostic odds ratios, bivariate Reitsma models, meta-regression, and SROC; meta4diag and bamdit provide Bayesian bivariate models; diagmeta models multiple cutoffs within a study; CopulaREMADA handles more complex dependence; DTAplots and dmetatools support plots and supplementary SROC inference. Verify TP/FP/FN/TN, thresholds, spectrum, reference standards, and continuity corrections before selecting a model.

23.5.2 General Effect Sizes and Prognostic Meta-Analysis

meta, metafor, and rmeta provide fixed/random effects, heterogeneity, meta-regression, and plots. metasens, robustmeta, netmeta, mvmeta, metaSEM, metavcov, robumeta, clubSandwich, bayesmeta, MetaStan, metamisc, metaSurvival, metagear, revtools, citationchaser, and PRISMA2020 cover sensitivity/bias analyses, network and multivariate meta-analysis, Bayesian models, prognostic synthesis, survival-curve reconstruction, and review workflows.

23.6 Survival and Event Outcomes

survival, prodlim, and rms support Kaplan–Meier and Cox models; survminer and ggsurvfit support presentation; flexsurv, rstpm2, and eha support flexible parametric models; cmprsk, riskRegression, mstate, and msm support competing risks and multistate models; timeROC and survivalROC support time-dependent ROC; pec and riskRegression support prediction error and calibration; glmnet, randomForestSRC, and ranger support penalized or machine-learning survival models; JMbayes2 and joineR support joint longitudinal-event models; powerSurvEpi, gsDesign, and rpact support design and power.

Baseline prognostic value, time-varying biomarkers, treatment-effect modification, and diagnostic accuracy are different estimands. Report time origin, censoring, competing events, prediction horizon, validation, and proportional-hazards checks.

23.7 Multivariate Analysis, Chemometrics, and High-Dimensional Biomarkers

FactoMineR, factoextra, psych, GPArotation, ade4, RGCCA, cluster, mclust, dbscan, fpc, compositions, robCompositions, rrcov, robustbase, mvoutlier, MVN, and mvnormtest support PCA, factor analysis, multi-table analysis, clustering, compositional data, robust covariance, and multivariate normality. pls, mdatools, chemometrics, prospectr, ChemoSpec, ChemoSpec2D, hyperSpec, and ALS support PLS/PCR, spectral preprocessing, chemometric calibration, and multivariate curve resolution.

For prediction, glmnet, e1071, kernlab, rpart, randomForest, ranger, xgboost, caret, tidymodels, and mlr3 support regularization, SVM, trees, ensembles, and complete resampling workflows. themis and ROSE support imbalance handling; iml and DALEX support explanations. Preprocessing, feature selection, and threshold selection must occur inside resampling folds to avoid leakage.

23.8 Regression, Clinical Prediction, and Utility

stats and MASS provide basic regression; logistf and brglm2 address separation; mgcv and rms support smooth and spline effects; geepack, lme4, nlme, and glmmTMB support repeated measures and clustering; emmeans, multcomp, sandwich, and clubSandwich support contrasts and robust SEs; pROC, yardstick, rms, riskRegression, and pec support model performance; pmcalibration supports calibration; and dcurves supports decision-curve analysis. Adding a marker should be assessed for discrimination, calibration, clinical thresholds, net benefit, overfitting, and external validation—not AUC increment alone.

23.9 Sample Size, Missing Data, and Statistical Support

ivdtools, presize, pROC, MKmisc, pmsampsize, WebPower, pwr, longpower, powerSurvEpi, gsDesign, and rpact support different sample-size and design problems. Partition total sample size into positive and negative targets, and account separately for prevalence, attrition, invalid samples, clustering, pairing, and multiple endpoints.

naniar and VIM diagnose missingness; mice, Amelia, missForest, and missMDA support imputation; outliers, EnvStats, nortest, rrcov, mvoutlier, and robustbase support distribution and outlier diagnostics; boot and rsample support resampling; multcomp and emmeans support multiplicity adjustments. Below-detection-limit results, instrument errors, missing visits, and random missing values are different mechanisms and should not all be treated as ordinary NA.

23.10 Data Management, Reporting, and Reproducibility

readr, readxl, haven, data.table, dplyr, and tidyr support import and data preparation. checkmate, pointblank, validate, and assertr support validation rules. broom, parameters, and performance tidy and audit model results. gtsummary, tableone, arsenal, and finalfit generate statistical tables. ggplot2, patchwork, and forestplot support visualization. knitr and rmarkdown create dynamic reports, renv and sessioninfo lock environments, and targets orchestrates reproducible pipelines.

A formal IVD project should preserve a read-only raw-data copy, data-generation rules, package versions, arguments, random seeds, exclusions, output objects, and the final report. CRAN availability is not the same as project-level software validation or regulatory approval.

23.11 Complete Review

23.11.1 Scope and Inclusion Rules

This review uses the following reproducible scope:

  1. Include packages available from CRAN before 2026-09-01 that directly target IVD/laboratory medicine or provide core statistical methods for IVD research. Foundational or recommended packages such as stats, survival, MASS, and nlme are included.
  2. Cover analytical performance, clinical performance, meta-analysis, survival analysis, multivariate analysis, and prediction modeling; one package may appear in several categories.
  3. Do not call every data-wrangling, plotting, database, or general statistics package an IVD package. Otherwise the set of all packages usable for IVD would have no finite boundary.
  4. Exclude packages released only through Bioconductor, GitHub, or other repositories, and packages withdrawn from CRAN. Many omics tools are hosted by Bioconductor and should be searched separately.

This is a thematic, broad directory as of the stated date, not a software-quality ranking or regulatory endorsement. CRAN availability, versions, defaults, and maintenance may change. A CRAN Task View is navigation by a defined topic, not a certification of the packages it lists.

23.12.6 Stability, Interference, Recovery, Lot Differences, and QC

CRAN has no single universal IVD package for these tasks. ivdtools provides stability regression, interference/recovery, dilution, lot bias, uncertainty, and Westgard QC components. General-purpose alternatives include stats, nlme, lme4, emmeans, multcomp, segmented, strucchange, changepoint, qcc, IQCC, qicharts2, SixSigma, spc, metRology, propagate, errors, and units. These support regression, ANOVA, repeated measures, mixed models, marginal comparisons, change points, control charts, and uncertainty propagation.

Stability, interference, and lot differences are not only comparisons of means. Time correlation, pairing, concentration-dependent bias, allowable error, and worst-case decisions must be expressed in the model and protocol.

23.14 Diagnostic Accuracy and General Meta-Analysis

23.14.1 Diagnostic-Accuracy Meta-Analysis

Pooling sensitivity and specificity separately ignores their dependence and threshold effects. Diagnostic meta-analysis often needs bivariate random-effects, HSROC, or multi-threshold models.

meta4diag Bayesian bivariate diagnostic meta-analysis Bayesian inference
bamdit JAGS-based Bayesian bivariate random-effects models Bayesian DTA

Before selecting a model, verify TP, FP, FN, TN, thresholds, spectrum, reference standard, zero-cell correction, multiple cutoffs, case-control sampling, and inter-study threshold differences.

23.14.2 General Effect Sizes, Prognosis, and IPD Meta-Analysis

meta, metafor, and rmeta provide fixed/random effects, heterogeneity, meta-regression, and plots. metasens and robustmeta support sensitivity and robust analyses; netmeta supports network meta-analysis; mvmeta, metaSEM, and metavcov support multivariate outcomes; robumeta and clubSandwich support dependent effects and robust variance; bayesmeta and MetaStan support Bayesian synthesis; metamisc supports prognostic studies; metaSurvival supports survival-curve reconstruction; metagear, revtools, citationchaser, and PRISMA2020 support review workflows.

Diagnostic performance, treatment effects, and prognostic effects are different problems even when all are called meta-analysis. IPD meta-analysis must model the study level explicitly and may combine with lme4, nlme, survival, or Bayesian hierarchical models.

23.16 Multivariate Analysis, Chemometrics, and High-Dimensional Biomarkers

23.16.1 Exploratory Multivariate Analysis

FactoMineR, factoextra, psych, GPArotation, ade4, and RGCCA support PCA, factor analysis, rotations, multiple-table analysis, and multi-block integration. cluster, mclust, dbscan, and fpc support clustering. compositions and robCompositions address compositional data. rrcov, robustbase, and mvoutlier support robust covariance and multivariate outliers. MVN, mvnormtest, and ICSNP support multivariate normality and inference.

PCA, clustering, and heatmaps explore structure but do not prove generalizable diagnostic performance. If variables are selected and AUC is reported on the same data, use nested resampling or independent data to correct selection bias.

Total sample size should be divided into positive and negative targets, with separate additions for prevalence, attrition, invalid samples, clustering, pairing, and multiple primary endpoints.

naniar and VIM inspect missingness; mice and Amelia support chained-equation or joint-model imputation; missForest and missMDA support nonlinear or multivariate imputation; outliers, EnvStats, and nortest support univariate distribution diagnostics; rrcov, mvoutlier, and robustbase support multivariate diagnostics; boot and rsample support resampling; multcomp and emmeans support multiplicity.

Below-detection-limit results, instrument errors, lost follow-up, and random missingness are different mechanisms. The imputation model should include the outcome, design variables, and auxiliary variables and should be compatible with the primary analysis.

A formal IVD project should retain a read-only raw-data copy, data-generation rules, package versions, function arguments, random seeds, exclusion records, output objects, and the final report. CRAN publication status is not project-level software validation or regulatory approval.
